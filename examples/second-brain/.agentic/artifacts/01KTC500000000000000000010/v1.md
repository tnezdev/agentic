# Reading Queue Practices Research Seed

## Question

How can a personal knowledge system keep a reading queue useful without turning it into a stagnant backlog?

## Why It Matters

Reading queues are easy to fill and hard to trust. A second-brain workspace needs a small, reviewable way to preserve useful reading intent without converting every interesting link into an obligation.

## Findings

- Separate items to read soon from someday/reference material so the active queue stays actionable.
- Capture why an item matters and what decision or project it supports; a URL alone is weak durable context.
- Review and prune the queue on a cadence so stale items become archived references, follow-up tasks, or deliberate deletions.
- The example should model the research loop and durable output, not prescribe a specific reading app.

## Tradeoffs

- A short queue is more actionable, but it drops some low-priority curiosity.
- Rich metadata improves retrieval, but too much structure makes capture feel expensive.
- Regular review keeps the queue trustworthy, but it adds maintenance work.

## Recommendation

Use a small active reading queue for near-term intent, a resource bucket for someday/reference items, and a weekly review step that promotes, archives, or deletes queued items. Replace this seed with a real recurring research question in your own workspace.
